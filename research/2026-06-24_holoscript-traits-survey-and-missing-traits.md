# HoloScript Traits — Survey & Valuable Missing Traits

**Date:** 2026-06-24
**Author:** Claude (claude-opus-4-8, branch `claude/holoscript-traits-research-x8003q`)
**Scope:** The HoloScript trait system as it exists today (`packages/core/src/traits/`),
the structural gap between trait *vocabulary* and trait *substance*, and a prioritized,
evidence-based list of genuinely-absent traits worth adding.
**Method:** Direct source reading + scripted counting over the local checkout
(`mcp.holoscript.net` not probed from this sandbox). All numbers reproduced from source.
**Status:** Research / decision input. Not a shipped code change.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Three findings, one recommendation.

1. **The trait system is enormous in breadth and shallow in depth.** The catalog declares
   **2,352 unique trait names** across **150 category arrays** (87 of them substantially
   populated). But the machine-readable registry that generators, validators, training, and
   Studio actually consume (`trait-registry.json`) has only **302 entries** — and of those,
   **8 carry a property schema, 24 carry compile hints, 1 carries a conflict rule, and 241 are
   classified `category: "other"`.** ~452 handler `*Trait.ts` files exist, but **92% of the
   declared vocabulary has no registry metadata at all.** This re-confirms — with fresh numbers —
   the prior finding that "~80–93% of traits are *declarations* not behavior"
   (`research/2026-06-15_trait-parity-and-tsx-deprecation.md`).

2. **The most valuable "missing traits" are mostly not new names — they are depth on names that
   already exist.** A generator can emit `@cuttable` or `@patrol` and the parser won't warn, but
   nothing validates the trait's properties, declares what it conflicts with, or supplies a
   compile hint. The highest-leverage work is closing the metadata + handler gap on the vocabulary
   already shipped, not minting more vocabulary. (See the parity backlog of ~50 behavioral/render
   handlers in the 2026-06-15 doc, and the OVERCLAIMED algorithm-trait family in
   `research/2026-05-24_deep-ratchet-trait-solver.md`.)

3. **There are, however, genuinely-absent capability domains** — concepts with no trait name at
   all, where the platform's own strategy says it most needs one. The standout is **agent/LLM
   safety**: HoloScript ships a 14-strong agent/LLM trait family yet has **no** trait for token
   budgeting, prompt-injection guarding, tool-call allowlisting, or output verification. The
   second is **server-authoritative networking**: rich client-side sync traits exist, but nothing
   expresses authority/reconciliation/prediction.

**Recommendation:** Treat "missing traits" as two tracks. **Track A (depth, highest ROI):**
backfill registry metadata (properties/conflicts/compileHints) for the existing handler-backed
traits so generation and validation become trustworthy. **Track B (net-new, strategically
gated):** add the small set of genuinely-absent traits below, leading with the agent-safety
family because it is the one gap that sits directly on top of what the ecosystem ships and sells.

## 2. The Trait System As It Exists (three layers)

The system is best understood as three stacked layers, each thinner than the one below:

| Layer | Artifact | Count | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | `traits/constants/*.ts` (`VR_TRAITS`) | **2,352 names / 150 arrays** | Parser/LSP/linter accept the name without an "unknown trait" warning |
| **Registry metadata** | `traits/trait-registry.json` (generated by `scripts/generate-trait-registry.ts`) | **302 entries** | Properties schema, compile hints, composition/conflict rules — consumed by Studio API, training trait-mappings, generators |
| **Handler behavior** | `traits/*Trait.ts` | **~452 files** | Actual runtime/compile behavior |

The layers are **not aligned**: vocabulary ⊃ handlers ⊅ registry. You can have a name with a
handler but no registry entry, or a registry entry that is a bare skeleton. Concretely, in the
registry today:

- **8 / 302** entries have a non-empty `properties` array
- **24 / 302** have `compileHints`
- **1 / 302** has a `conflicts` rule
- **241 / 302** are `category: "other"` (unclassified)

So the machine-readable surface that a *generator* or *validator* relies on is almost entirely
empty even where rich handler code exists. The generator (`generate-trait-registry.ts`) is
producing skeletons, not extracting the metadata the handlers already encode.

### Where the breadth lives (top categories by vocabulary size)

```
239  universal-service     78  security-crypto      75  facial-expression
62   npc-roles             51  character-pipeline    48  animals
46   data-visualization    42  creatures-mythical    41  intelligence-behavior
39   cooking-food          39  education-learning    37  architecture-realestate
37   magic-fantasy         37  music-performance     37  sports-fitness
```

Many of the largest categories (`animals`, `facial-expression`, `creatures-mythical`,
`cooking-food`, `magic-fantasy`, `npc-roles`, `character-pipeline`) are almost pure vocabulary —
names a generator can pattern-match against, with little or no dedicated handler. That is fine for
*authoring suggestions*; it is not the same as a capability.

## 3. The Dominant Gap — Breadth Without Depth

This is already established doctrine (2026-06-15 parity research; 2026-05-24 deep-ratchet;
2026-05-12 D.040 recalibration), so this section confirms rather than re-derives. The new
contribution is the registry-metadata measurement above. The practical consequences:

- **Validation can't catch property errors.** With 8/302 property schemas, `validate_holoscript`
  cannot tell a model it passed a bad value to a trait it just emitted.
- **Composition is unguarded.** With 1 conflict rule total, nothing stops a generator from
  stacking mutually-exclusive traits (e.g. `@kinematic` + `@soft_body`).
- **Compile targets are mostly unhinted.** 24/302 compile hints means most traits carry no
  guidance for the per-target compilers.

The known-real depth is concentrated in: the Tier-1 numeric **solvers** (genuinely real FEM/CFD/
FDTD/MD/PBD per deep-ratchet), the **networking/sync** traits (`NetworkedTrait`, `CRDTRoomTrait`,
`SyncTierTrait`), core **interaction/physics** (`GrabbableTrait`, `RigidbodyTrait`, `JointTrait`),
and the **agent/LLM** family below. Everything else trends toward declaration.

## 4. Genuinely-Absent Valuable Traits (net-new)

These are concepts with **no vocabulary name at all** (verified against the flattened 2,352-name
vocab), ranked by strategic fit to what the ecosystem actually ships.

### 4.1 — Agent / LLM safety family  ★ highest strategic value

The ecosystem is agent-native (MCP server, multi-agent, RAG, fine-tune all shipped as traits:
`LLMAgentTrait`, `MultiAgentTrait`, `RAGKnowledgeTrait`, `AgentMemoryTrait`, `InferenceTrait`,
`FineTuneTrait`, `PromptTemplateTrait`, `ModerationTrait`, `LocalLLMTrait`, …). Yet **none of
these express how to make an agent safe or bounded.** Absent names:

| Proposed trait | Gap it closes |
|---|---|
| `@token_budget` | Hard per-invocation / per-session token + cost ceiling on an LLM-driven object (the ecosystem already enforces a `$100/day` GPU cap *out of band* — there is no in-language trait for it) |
| `@guardrail` | Declarative input/output content + policy gating around an `@llm_agent` (distinct from `@moderation`, which is content moderation of *user* media, not agent I/O) |
| `@prompt_injection_guard` | Detect/strip injection in untrusted text an agent ingests — directly relevant to the "external content" trust boundary the platform already worries about |
| `@tool_call_scoped` | Allowlist/deny the tools an `@llm_agent` may call (the MCP server already has `security/tool-scopes.ts` — there is no trait that surfaces it) |
| `@output_verified` | Bind the existing `@holoscript/ai-validator` (Levenshtein hallucination detection) to an agent's output as a composable trait — the capability ships as a *package* but is invisible as a *trait* |

This family is the single best net-new investment: it is small, it sits on top of capabilities
that already exist (tool-scopes, ai-validator, the cost-cap policy), and it makes the platform's
core product — agents — describable as *safe by construction* in the language itself.

### 4.2 — Server-authoritative networking

Client-side sync is real (`NetworkedTrait`, delta-compression, CRDT). But the 2026-06-15 parity
audit scored multiplayer 5/10 specifically for **"no server authority."** No vocabulary exists
for it. Absent names: `@server_authoritative`, `@client_predicted`, `@reconciled`,
`@lag_compensated`, `@interest_managed` (interest-management code exists but is "never called"),
`@rollback_netcode`. These are the traits that separate a demo from an MMO, and the platform's
own roadmap names that axis as its weakest.

### 4.3 — Scene-understanding / AR semantic anchoring

Geometry-level AR traits exist (`PlaneDetectionTrait`, `MeshDetectionTrait`, `AnchorTrait`,
`RoomMeshTrait`). The *semantic* layer is missing: `@semantic_segmented`, `@object_anchored`
(anchor to a recognized object class, not a plane), `@slam_tracked`, `@occlusion_meshed`. This is
where consumer AR (Niantic LGM, ARKit scene-meaning) is heading and the 2026-04-23 geospatial
research already gestures at it.

### 4.4 — Modern GPU rendering primitives

`@gaussian_splat` and `@reflection_probe` exist; the rest of the contemporary pipeline does not:
`@virtualized_geometry` / `@nanite_lod` (the 2026-06-15 audit cites a "12–20× polygon gap vs
AAA"), `@temporal_upscaled` (DLSS/FSR-class), `@light_probe` (GI baking), `@sdf_collision`. These
are large to implement but each directly attacks the rendering score (3.5/10).

### 4.5 — Smaller, well-bounded economy & data primitives

Lower priority but cheap and self-contained: `@escrow`, `@fractional_ownership`, `@staking`,
`@dao_governed` (economy — `@royalty` and `WalletTrait`/`NFTTrait` exist, the rest of a real
on-chain economy doesn't); `@event_sourced`, `@idempotent`, `@cqrs` (data — `@saga`,
`@backpressure`, `@change_data_capture` already present, so this completes a half-built set).

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Track A — Backfill registry depth (do first, highest ROI).** Extend
   `generate-trait-registry.ts` to extract `properties`, `conflicts`, and `compileHints` from the
   ~452 existing handlers instead of emitting skeletons. Target: move "8/302 with properties"
   toward "every handler-backed trait has a property schema." This makes `validate_holoscript` and
   `suggest_traits` trustworthy with zero new vocabulary.
2. **Track B1 — Ship the agent-safety family (§4.1).** Five small traits wrapping capabilities
   that already exist. Founder-gate `@guardrail`/`@prompt_injection_guard` policy semantics
   (shared-doctrine / safety surface).
3. **Track B2 — Spec server-authority netcode (§4.2)** as the prerequisite for any MMO claim;
   coordinate with the existing parity backlog rather than opening a parallel one.
4. **Defer §4.3–4.5** until A and B1 land; they are real but not on the critical path.

## 6. What Remains After This Plan (Completeness Gaps)

This is research, not a shipped change. Deliberately left unaddressed:

- **No code was written or validated.** No traits were added; no `suggest_traits`/
  `validate_holoscript` calls were made (MCP not probed from this sandbox). All counts are static
  source analysis and may differ from the live registry the MCP server serves.
- **The handler-coverage matcher is approximate.** Section 2's handler count is a filename count;
  a name-normalization pass to map every vocabulary entry to its handler (or prove none exists)
  was not done — so "92% no registry metadata" is exact, but "category X has 0 handlers" is
  indicative, not audited per-name.
- **Net-new trait list is not exhaustive.** §4 probed a curated set of high-value domains for
  *absence*; it is not a complete enumeration of everything missing. Education, healthcare,
  robotics, and IoT categories were not probed for gaps.
- **No prioritization by usage data.** Ranking is by strategic fit (per INTENT/NORTH_STAR posture
  and prior audits), not by which traits agents/users actually request — that telemetry was not
  consulted.
- **Track A effort is unestimated.** Whether handler metadata is mechanically extractable or needs
  per-trait authoring is unknown until a spike on `generate-trait-registry.ts` is run.
